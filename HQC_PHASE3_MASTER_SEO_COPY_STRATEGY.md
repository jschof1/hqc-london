# High Quality Clean Phase 3 Master SEO and Copy Strategy

Date: 20 May 2026

Status: Strategic copy and SEO planning document for Pedro/Jack review before implementation.

Canonical site: https://highqualityclean.co.uk

## Executive Summary

High Quality Clean should stop leading with luxury/wellness/concierge language and reposition as a London-first cleaning partner for three practical buyer groups:

1. Property and Facilities
2. Homes
3. Commercial and Wellness

The hierarchy matters. Property and Facilities is the lead growth route because the Drive research identifies it as the biggest revenue opportunity and the largest current website gap. Homes remains the current revenue base and must be sharpened. Commercial and Wellness remains useful, but narrower and proof-dependent.

The new core positioning should be:

> Cleaning support for London homes, property teams, and premium spaces that need to stay ready.

This phrase is not trying to sound clever. It does four jobs:

- Names the category: cleaning support.
- Names the buyers: homes, property teams, premium spaces.
- States the outcome: stay ready.
- Leaves room for premium positioning through standards, not decorative language.

The homepage should become a routing and trust page, not a brand mood page. The most important first-screen job is to help the visitor understand:

- what HQC cleans
- who HQC serves
- why HQC is reliable
- which service route they should choose
- what action to take next

The first wave of copy should prioritise:

1. Property and Facilities Hub
2. Block Management Cleaning
3. Estate and Lettings Agents
4. Void and Post-Tenancy Cleaning
5. Developer Handover Cleaning
6. Partners Portfolio Programme
7. Homepage Rewrite
8. Homes Hub
9. End of Tenancy Cleaning Wandsworth
10. Post-Renovation Cleaning

This is not just a content rewrite. It is a positioning correction, site-architecture correction, and conversion-flow correction.

# 1. What We Know From The Research

## 1.1 Phase 1 Findings

The Drive document shows that the site has a visibility and click-choice problem.

Known performance:

- 371 clicks from 35,725 impressions over 16 months.
- Site-wide CTR around 1.04%.
- Non-branded CTR around 0.37%.
- Branded CTR around 8.0%.
- Homepage receives 27,767 impressions, around 91% of total visibility.
- Average UK position is around 30.
- UK is 80% of clicks and the only commercially relevant market.
- Mobile converts 2.6x better than desktop but receives only one third of impressions.
- Canonical fixes have already reduced around 70 URL variants to 38 actual topic pages.

Interpretation:

The site is being seen but not chosen. The canonical and redirect fixes mean the remaining problem is mainly content, intent, message clarity, page architecture, and conversion confidence.

1. Property and facilities decision-makers

3. Boutique commercial and wellness operators

The top service categories to grow are:

1. Regular residential cleaning
2. Move in / move out / end of tenancy cleaning

- The client wants a cleaner or team they can rely on without managing everything themselves.
- The property needs a proper reset after building works, neglect, or before maintenance can begin.

These pains should drive copy far more than abstract luxury language.

# 2. External SEO, CRO, and Competitor Evidence

## 2.1 Google Search Guidance

Google’s SEO guidance supports the direction of this rewrite:

- Page titles should be unique, clear, concise, and accurately describe the page.
- Snippets are often drawn from page content, so visible page copy matters for CTR.
- Helpful content should be written primarily for people, show expertise, provide complete information, and satisfy the reader’s goal.
- Structured data should describe visible page content and should be accurate.
- LocalBusiness structured data can help Google understand business details, but schema cannot compensate for weak, generic page copy.

Phase 3 implication:

Each important page needs a clear title, one primary search intent, a plain H1, specific service copy, visible trust proof, and a reason to enquire. We should not build thin service-location pages just because a keyword exists.

## 2.2 CRO and UX Guidance

Baymard’s homepage/category UX research repeatedly points to product/service finding as a core conversion problem: if users cannot quickly find the relevant category or route, they leave or lose confidence.

Phase 3 implication:

The homepage should behave like a decision gateway. The three-segment section is not decorative. It is the homepage’s conversion mechanism:

1. Property and Facilities
2. Homes
3. Commercial and Wellness

The section should appear early, after hero/trust proof, before brand story, testimonials, or broad service lists.

### Premium Home Cleaning Competitors

### End of Tenancy Competitors

Competitors often overclaim with “deposit-back guarantee.” HQC should be more careful.

Better language:

- inspection-ready
- checkout-ready
- agent/inventory-focused
- detailed move-out clean
- fixed quote
- re-clean support where cleaning-related issues are identified

Important legal/compliance note:

Government guidance around the Tenant Fees Act means copy should avoid implying that tenants are legally required to buy a professional clean. GOV.UK landlord guidance says landlords can withhold for reasonable cleaning costs where appropriate, but not reasonable wear and tear. Therefore HQC should avoid hard “guaranteed full deposit back” claims unless legally reviewed and operationally backed.

# 3. Final Phase 3 Positioning

## 3.1 Old Positioning Problem

The current site often leads with:

- luxury
- profound care
- perfected detail
- wellness
- concierge care
- emotional reset

These are not bad ideas, but they are poor first-screen conversion anchors because they do not quickly answer:

- What do you clean?
- Who is this for?
- Is this right for my situation?
- Can I trust you?
- What do I do next?

## 3.2 New Positioning

Recommended master position:

> Cleaning support for London homes, property teams, and premium spaces that need to stay ready.

Expanded version:

> High Quality Clean helps London property teams, households, and selected commercial spaces keep homes, managed properties, handovers, and client-facing environments clean, presentable, and ready without chasing standards.

## 3.3 Message Pillars

### Pillar 1: Ready Spaces

The strongest outcome is not “sparkling clean.” It is readiness.

Ready for:

- viewing
- handover
- occupation
- inspection
- guests
- residents
- staff
- clients
- regular maintenance

### Pillar 2: Fewer Standards To Chase

The hidden buyer pain is management burden.

The buyer does not only want cleaning. They want not to chase cleaners, clarify missed details, apologise to tenants, explain poor presentation to landlords, or supervise the work themselves.

### Pillar 3: Detail Without Damage

HQC’s premium proof should come from careful handling:

- delicate finishes
- marble
- fixtures
- fittings
- high ceilings
- expensive furnishings
- post-build dust
- high-touch areas
- communal spaces

### Pillar 4: Clear Scope

Generic cleaning copy underperforms because buyers do not know what they are getting.

HQC should repeatedly say:

- clear scope
- agreed brief
- fixed or scoped quote
- service notes
- access details
- priorities confirmed
- add-ons identified upfront

### Pillar 5: Trust and Reliability

Trust proof must appear early and often:

- insured
- vetted
- DBS-checked if true
- director-led oversight if true
- quality checks
- references/case studies
- Google reviews
- clear contact details
- public landline

Do not recommend FAQ schema for Google rich results on a commercial cleaning site. FAQ content may still be useful for users and AI/LLM extraction, but not as a Google rich-result play.

## 12.6 Content Quality Gates

Avoid thin local pages. If service-location pages are created, they need:

- unique local context
- specific service copy
- local buyer situation
- relevant nearby areas/internal links
- proof if available
- useful FAQ
- clear CTA

Do not mass-generate location pages with swapped place names.

## What Should Not Be Built First

The following should not lead Phase 3:

- more generic location pages without stronger copy and proof
- Well-Clean as a front-line offer
- concierge laundry as a major navigation item
- specialist cleaning as a vague catch-all
- comparison-style pages unless a real competitor-comparison strategy is intentionally chosen later

The competitor lesson here is not that HQC should copy competitor pages. The lesson is that competitors win because they make the buyer’s route obvious. HQC can beat that by combining route clarity with stronger standards, better property/facilities positioning, and a more premium-but-practical voice.
